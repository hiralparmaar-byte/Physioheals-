Why standard desks don’t fit

Seat depth

A deep seat presses behind the knee (the popliteal area) when your legs are shorter.

Pelvic tension

Long meetings keep pelvic floor muscles tight. Small position changes help.

Narrow shoulders

Standard keyboards make smaller frames reach wider, which loads the neck and wrists.

Five stress points

Neck and eyes

Top of the screen at eye level. A low laptop pushes the head forward.

Shoulders and bra line

Narrow shoulders reach further for a standard keyboard. Keep elbows near your sides and consider a compact keyboard.

Wrists

Keep wrists neutral to reduce pressure on the median nerve.

Pelvis and lower back

Sit back with a small lumbar support. Stand every 30 minutes.

Knees and feet

Leave two finger-widths behind the knee. Use a footrest if your feet dangle.

Desk micro-routines

Conditions we treat

Thoracic kyphosis and bra-strap pressure

Rounded upper back with strap-line soreness. Care combines chest opening, mid-back strengthening and a fit check.

Pelvic floor tension and tailbone load

Long sitting can keep pelvic muscles tight. Care focuses on breathing, release and sitting changes.

Postpartum return to desk

Gradual core and pelvic rehab with workday pacing and feeding-break stretches.

Narrow shoulder span and repetitive strain

Smaller frames reach further for standard peripherals. Care covers nerve glides and desk setup.

Sacroiliac joint instability

One-sided pelvic or buttock pain from sitting. Care builds hip and deep core control.
